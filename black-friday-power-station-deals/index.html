How to tell a real deal

  • Look at price per kWh, not the discount. Brand "list prices" are often far above what anyone pays, so "50% off" can still be an ordinary price. A 2 kWh unit at £807 is £395 per kWh, whatever it says it was.
  • Work out the size first. A bargain 1 kWh unit keeps a fridge-freezer going for less than a day. Three days of essentials needs about 5 kWh.
  • Check the delivery date. Some sale prices are for pre-orders. Anker's C1000 Gen 2 ships from 19 December and its F3000 from 2 January, which matters if you're buying for this winter's storms.
  • Solar bundles are only worth it if you'll use the panel. Panels make little in a UK winter, so in a December power cut you'll mostly be charging from the mains before a storm.
  • Buy from the brand or a big UK retailer. You get a UK plug, the full warranty and, for most online orders, 14 days to change your mind after delivery.
  • Ask about price guarantees. Some brands refund the difference if the price drops soon after you buy. Check the sale page on the day.
