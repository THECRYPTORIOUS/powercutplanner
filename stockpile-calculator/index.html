    Where these numbers come from

    • Water: the government's Be Ready checklist says 3 litres of drinking water per person per day. Its Prepare advice says 10 litres per person per day is more comfortable, because it also covers basic cooking and hygiene. Babies count as a person here, to cover washing and making up feeds.
    • Pets: dogs and cats usually drink about 40 to 60 ml per kg of body weight a day. We use 60 ml to be safe, so a 20 kg dog needs about 1.2 litres a day.
    • Food: rough daily needs of about 2,250 calories for adults and teenagers, 1,600 for children aged 4 to 12 and 1,000 for toddlers, based on NHS and SACN guidance. Babies are fed milk, so they're not counted.

    Tips that make a stockpile actually work

    • Pick food you can eat cold. In a power cut an electric hob and microwave won't work. Ring-pull tins, cereal bars, crackers, nut butter, dried fruit and long-life milk all need no cooking.
    • Keep a manual tin opener with the tins.
    • Buy what you already eat and rotate it: use the oldest tins in your normal cooking and replace them, so nothing goes out of date.
    • Babies: the Be Ready advice says ready-to-feed formula is best, because you may not be able to boil water to make up powdered formula safely. Keep nappies and wipes too.
    • Medicines: keep at least a week of any regular prescriptions, and a written list of doses.
    • Never cook indoors on a camping stove or barbecue. They give off carbon monoxide.
