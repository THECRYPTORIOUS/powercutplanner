      Before a power cut

        Common questions

        Why do I need more than my appliances add up to?

        A power station stores energy as DC and converts it to 230 V mains, which wastes some of it. We allow 15% for that. On top, the power station uses about 15 W of its own whenever its plug sockets are switched on, around 0.36 kWh a day. That matters most when you're only running a fridge. Things charged from the USB or 12 V sockets, like phones or a 12 V camping fridge, don't need the plug sockets on.

        My smart meter says I use more than this. Why?

        Your meter counts the whole house, including everything on standby: boiler controls, alarms, chargers, the TV box and so on. Together that's often 100 W or more, about 2.5 kWh a day, even when you're out. A power station only runs what you plug into it, so you only need to cover the things you've ticked here.

        Will a power station run my gas or oil boiler?

        Often, yes. A gas boiler's controls and pump draw roughly 100 W, and an oil boiler a bit more while the burner is firing. But most boilers are wired into a fused spur rather than a plug, so an electrician needs to fit a proper plug-in connection first. Never wire a power station into your home's circuits yourself.

        Can I recharge it with a solar panel during a long power cut?

        Yes, but go easy on the maths in winter. A UK solar panel produces only a small share of its rated watts on a grey December day, so treat solar as a top-up rather than a replacement for battery size.

        Is a petrol generator a better idea?

        It's cheaper per kWh, but it's noisy and needs fuel stored safely. Above all, a petrol generator must never be run indoors or in a garage because of carbon monoxide. A battery power station is silent and safe to use inside.
