What size power station do you need for a power cut?

Updated 15 November 2026

Short answer

To keep a fridge-freezer, Wi-Fi, lights and phones going for the three days the government suggests planning for, you need about 4 kWh. A 1 kWh unit covers those for about 18 hours, and a 2 kWh unit for about a day and a half.

The three sizes

About 1 kWh: the essentials for a day

Enough for Wi-Fi, lights and phone charging for several days, or a fridge-freezer for about a day. Light enough for one person to carry, at roughly 11 to 13 kg.

About 2 kWh: a day and a half of normal essentials

Runs a fridge-freezer plus Wi-Fi, lights and phones for about 37 hours. A good middle ground if power cuts where you live usually last hours rather than days.

3 to 4 kWh: three days

The size you need for the full three days with a fridge-freezer included. These are heavy, and the biggest come on wheels.

What to look for

  • Capacity in Wh decides how long it lasts. Work out your daily use first, then multiply by the days you want to cover.
  • Output in W decides what it can run at once. A standard kettle needs about 3,000 W, which rules out most units under 3 kWh.
  • LFP battery chemistry (lithium iron phosphate, sometimes written LiFePO4) lasts thousands of charge cycles. The main brands' current models use it.
  • UPS mode switches to battery within milliseconds when the power goes, so a router or computer stays on without restarting.
  • Expandability lets you start smaller and add a battery later, which can work out cheaper than buying the biggest unit up front.
