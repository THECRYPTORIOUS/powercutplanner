How long will a power station run a fridge-freezer?

Updated 15 November 2026

Short answer

A typical 1 kWh power station runs an average UK fridge-freezer for about 30 hours. A 2 kWh unit lasts about 2½ days, and a 4 kWh unit about 5 days, if the fridge-freezer is the only thing plugged in.

How much power a fridge-freezer uses

A fridge-freezer doesn't run constantly. Its compressor switches on and off to hold the temperature, so what matters is how much energy it uses across a whole day. A typical modern fridge-freezer uses roughly 200 to 300 kWh a year, which works out at about 0.5 to 0.8 kWh a day. We use 0.7 kWh a day as a middle figure.

You can find your own figure on the energy label, which shows kWh per year (kWh/annum). Divide it by 365. For example, 255 kWh a year is 0.7 kWh a day.

Run times by power station size

These figures assume 85% of the rated capacity is usable, because power stations lose some energy converting battery power to 230 V mains.

Will it handle the start-up surge?

When the compressor kicks in, a fridge-freezer briefly draws several times its normal running power. Mid-size power stations rated at 1,000 W or more handle this comfortably, and all the models in our size guide are rated at 1,800 W or more.

Make the battery last longer

  • Keep the doors shut. Every opening lets cold air out and makes the compressor work harder.
  • Keep the freezer full. A full freezer holds its temperature much longer than a half-empty one. Bottles of water fill gaps well.
  • Don't put it in a hot spot. A fridge-freezer next to a radiator or oven uses more energy.
