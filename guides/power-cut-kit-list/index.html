Power cut kit list: what to have ready at home

Updated 18 November 2026 · UK

Short answer

For most homes the basics are cheap: a torch with spare batteries, a charged power bank, a battery or wind-up radio, warm layers and blankets, 3 days of water (3 litres per person per day) and food that needs no cooking. Add a battery carbon monoxide alarm if you'll use anything that burns fuel.

This list follows the government's emergency supplies advice and UK fire service power cut guidance, with the practical bits people forget. Light

  • Head torch

    Keeps both hands free for finding the fuse box, cooking or getting kids to bed. Torches are safer than candles, and fire services ask people to use them instead.

    Look for: a rechargeable one that also takes ordinary batteries, so a flat battery doesn't leave you in the dark.

  • LED lantern

    Lights a whole room, so the family can sit together without everyone holding a torch.

    Look for: battery or USB-rechargeable, with a low setting that runs for days.

  • Spare batteries

  • Battery candles

Phones and news

  • Power bank

    Keeps phones going when the sockets don't. The government's emergency list includes a charged power bank for phones and compatible medical devices.

    Look for: about 20,000 mAh, which charges a typical phone 3 to 4 times. Top it up every few months.

    See options on Amazon

  • Battery or wind-up radio

    When mobile signal and broadband go down with the power, the radio is how you get updates. The government recommends a battery or wind-up FM radio, with your local stations' frequencies written down.

    Look for: FM as well as DAB, and a hand crank or batteries rather than mains only.

  • USB car charger

    Your car is a big battery on the drive. A plug-in USB charger lets you top up phones from it.

    Look for: a fast-charging one with USB-C. Only run the engine outdoors, never in a closed garage.

Staying warm

  • Hot water bottle and flask

    Your boiler won't run without electricity, even on gas. A flask of hot water filled before a forecast storm means hot drinks and a hot water bottle later.

    Look for: a 1 litre vacuum flask that keeps water hot for 12 hours or more.

    See flasks on Amazon

  • Sleeping bags and blankets

    The government's list includes blankets, sleeping bags and warm clothing. A house can get cold fast in a winter power cut.

    Look for: sleeping bags rated for cold nights, and thermal layers, hats and gloves for everyone.

Food, water and keeping things cold

  • Water

    The government says to keep bottled water for 3 days, at 3 litres per person per day just for drinking. That's 9 litres each, plus extra for cooking and washing. Our stockpile calculator works out your family's total.

    Look for: a 10 or 20 litre water container you can fill and keep, or multipacks of bottled water.

    See options on Amazon

  • Manual tin opener

    Tinned food is no use if your only opener is electric.

    Look for: a sturdy, simple one. Keep it with the tins.

  • Cool box and freezer blocks

    Keep the fridge and freezer shut as long as you can. If the cut drags on, a cool box packed with freezer blocks keeps milk and medicines cold.

    Look for: a well-insulated box and a stack of freezer blocks kept in the freezer.

  • Fridge thermometer

    Tells you whether food is still safe once the power comes back, instead of guessing.

    Look for: a cheap dial or digital thermometer that sits inside the fridge.

Safety

Never use a barbecue, camping stove, gas heater or generator indoors or in a garage. They give off carbon monoxide, which you can't see or smell.

  • Battery carbon monoxide alarm

    If you use a gas or paraffin heater, or anything that burns fuel, a CO alarm is essential. Fire services advise one in every room with a gas or paraffin heater.

    Look for: a battery-powered alarm that meets BS EN 50291, so it still works in a power cut.

    See options on Amazon

  • First aid kit

    On every official emergency list. Add a week of any regular medication.

    Look for: a household kit, plus a written list of your medicines and doses.

  • Camping stove (outdoors only)

    If your hob is electric, a camping stove lets you boil water and heat food. Only ever use it outside: camping stoves, barbecues and generators give off carbon monoxide.

    Look for: a single-burner gas stove, and change gas cartridges outside.

Free things to do as well

  • Save 105 in your phone and write it on paper. It's the free number to report a power cut in England, Scotland and Wales. In Northern Ireland it's 03457 643 643.
  • Join the Priority Services Register if anyone at home relies on medical equipment, is elderly or has a disability. It's free, through your energy supplier or network operator.
  • Keep some cash, because card machines go down too.
  • Know where your fuse box is, and read what to check if it's just your house.
