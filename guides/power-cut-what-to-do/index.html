Power cut? What to do right now

Updated 16 November 2026 · England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

Short answer

Call 105 to report it (free, England, Scotland and Wales). In Northern Ireland call NIE Networks on 03457 643 643. Keep the fridge and freezer shut, use torches rather than candles, and never use a barbecue, camping stove or generator indoors. If the cut lasts more than 12 hours you're usually owed compensation automatically.

    1. Check whether it's just your home

    Look at your fuse box (the consumer unit). If a switch has flipped down, unplug anything that might have caused it and switch it back up. If your neighbours' lights are out too, it's a fault on the network and only your local network operator can fix it. Your energy supplier can't.

    2. Report it

    • England, Scotland and Wales: call 105. It's free from most phones and puts you through to the company that runs the power lines in your area, whoever you pay for electricity. You can also check live outages at powercut105.com.
    • Northern Ireland: call NIE Networks on 03457 643 643. 105 doesn't cover Northern Ireland.
    • Fallen power lines or a damaged substation: stay well away and call 105. If someone is in danger, call 999.

    3. Stay safe

    Never use a barbecue, camping stove or petrol generator indoors, or in a garage or tent. They give off carbon monoxide, which you can't see or smell and which can kill. A battery power station doesn't burn fuel, so it's safe to use inside.

    • Use torches or battery lanterns rather than candles.
    • Switch off or unplug cookers, heaters and other appliances that could come back on unattended. Leave one light switched on so you know when the power returns.
    • Check on neighbours who are older, disabled or live alone.
    • Keep your phone charged from a power bank or the car, and keep calls short so the battery lasts. Mobile networks can be patchy in a long cut.

    4. Fridge and freezer: how long food stays safe

    The Food Standards Agency says a power cut of under 4 hours is unlikely to affect chilled or frozen food. For longer cuts:

    • Keep the doors shut. A fridge stays cold for up to about 4 hours.
    • Freezer: food should stay frozen for up to about 48 hours in a full freezer, or about 24 hours if it's half full. It varies by model.
    • If the fridge reaches 8°C or above, throw away food that needs chilling after 4 hours, unless the power comes back within that time.
    • In cold weather, below 8°C, chilled food can go outside in a sealed box, out of the sun and away from animals.
    • Eat fridge food first, then defrosted food that doesn't need cooking, then tins and dried food.

    5. Keeping warm

    Most gas boilers stop in a power cut, because their controls and pump need electricity. Wear layers, close the curtains, and keep everyone in one room. See whether a power station can run your boiler.

    6. If you rely on electricity for medical equipment

    Sign up to the free Priority Services Register with your network operator and your supplier. People on it get priority updates and extra support during a power cut. If you use equipment such as a home oxygen concentrator, a CPAP machine or a stair lift, tell 105 when you report the cut.

    7. Compensation you may be owed

    In England, Scotland and Wales, Ofgem sets payments your network operator must make if your power isn't back on in time. The figures below are Ofgem's current amounts for homes.

    SituationPayment
    Normal weather, fewer than 5,000 homes affected, off for more than 12 hours£100, then £45 for each further 12 hours
    Normal weather, 5,000 or more homes affected, off for more than 24 hours£100, then £45 for each further 12 hours (up to £400)
    Storm category 1, off for 24 hours£90, then £45 for every 6 hours after (up to £2,235)
    Storm category 2, off for 48 hours£90, then £45 for every 6 hours after (up to £2,235)
    More than 4 cuts of 3 hours or longer between April and MarchAn extra £100. You have to claim this one

    The main payments are automatic, so you don't need to claim. If nothing has arrived after a few months, contact your network operator. Northern Ireland has its own rules, set by the Utility Regulator. Check the details on Ofgem's power cut page.

    8. Next time

    The government's Be Ready campaign asks every household to plan for three days without power. A battery power station can keep a fridge-freezer, Wi-Fi, lights and phones going, but the size you need depends on what you'd run and for how long.
