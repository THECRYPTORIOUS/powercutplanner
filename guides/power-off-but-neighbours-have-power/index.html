Power off but your neighbours have power? What to check

Updated 18 November 2026 · England, Scotland and Wales

Short answer

If the street lights and next door still have power, the problem is probably in your home. Check your fuse box first, then your prepayment meter if you have one. If both look fine, call 105. The main fuse and the cable into your home belong to your network operator, and a fault there can cut off your house while next door stays on.

1. Check your fuse box

Your fuse box (the consumer unit) is usually near the meter, under the stairs or in a cupboard by the front door. It has a main switch, one or two RCDs (switches with a small test button marked T), and a row of smaller circuit breakers.

  • A switch has flipped down: push it back up. If only part of the house was off, a single circuit breaker is the usual cause.
  • It trips again straight away: unplug everything on that circuit, reset the switch, then plug things back in one at a time. The one that trips it is the faulty appliance. Things that heat water and anything outside that's got wet are common culprits.
  • It still trips with everything unplugged: the fault is probably in the wiring. Leave it off and call a registered electrician rather than resetting it again and again.
  • Older fuse box with fuse wire or cartridge fuses: a blown fuse has to be replaced with one of the same rating. If you're not sure how, get an electrician.

2. Check your prepayment meter

If you pay as you go, check the meter's display before anything else.

  • On zero: top up, or switch on your emergency credit. It's a small amount, often £5 to £10, and it comes off your next top-up.
  • Friendly credit: energy suppliers must offer credit that keeps your electricity on overnight, at weekends and on bank holidays, when top-up shops may be shut. If your power goes off during those hours and you still have credit, something else is wrong.
  • Credit on the meter but no power: call your energy supplier (the number is on your bill or app), not 105. The law says they must get you back on supply within 3 hours on a working day or 4 hours on other days, counted from the start of their next working hours if you call outside them. If they miss it, they owe you compensation.

3. Everything looks fine? Call 105

Next to your meter there's a sealed black or grey box containing the main fuse, called the cut-out. It belongs to your network operator, the company that runs the power lines in your area, not you or your supplier. If the main fuse blows, or the cable from the street to your home fails, your house goes off while the neighbours stay on.

Homes on the same street aren't always fed by the same cable, either. A network fault can take out some houses and leave others on, so "next door has power" doesn't rule out a power cut.

Call 105. It's free from most phones and puts you through to your network operator. It's their equipment, so they come out and fix it.

Never break the seals or pull the main fuse yourself. If you can smell burning or see scorching, sparks or smoke at the fuse box or meter, switch off at the main switch if it's safe to, keep away, and call 105. If there's a fire, call 999.

While you wait

A fault on your own cable can take a few hours to fix, the same as any other power cut. Keep the fridge and freezer shut, and use torches rather than candles. Our power cut guide covers food safety and the compensation you may be owed.
