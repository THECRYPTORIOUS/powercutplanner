Do plug-in solar panels work in a power cut?

Updated 16 November 2026

Short answer

No. A plug-in solar kit switches itself off as soon as the power goes off, even in bright sunshine, and stays off until the grid comes back. To keep things running in a power cut you need a battery that works on its own, such as a portable power station.

Why it switches off

A plug-in kit pushes power back through your wall socket into your home's wiring. When the grid is working, that's fine: your home uses the solar power first and takes the rest from the grid.

In a power cut, engineers may be working on the lines outside. If solar kits kept running, they could send electricity down lines that are meant to be dead. So the rules require every kit to detect that the grid has gone and shut down straight away. It's a safety feature, and you can't switch it off.

The same goes for the socket itself. With the grid off, the kit has no mains power to match, so it can't run your home on its own.

What does work in a power cut

A portable power station is a big rechargeable battery with normal 3-pin sockets. It isn't connected to your home's wiring, so it keeps working when the grid is down. Most can be recharged from their own folding solar panel, which plugs into the power station rather than the wall.

Can I have both?

Yes, and they work well together. Keep the power station plugged in to charge on sunny afternoons. Your home uses the solar power first, so the panels cover most of the charge. When a power cut comes, unplug the power station and plug your fridge-freezer, Wi-Fi and lamps into it.

Never plug a power station's output into a wall socket to try to power your home, and never use a cable with a plug on both ends. It's dangerous for you and for engineers on the lines. A whole-home backup needs a proper changeover switch fitted by an electrician.

What about kits with a battery?

Not allowed yet. The government's product rules for plug-in solar leave battery systems out for now, and kits have to warn you not to use them with battery storage. Even when plug-in batteries are allowed, storing power isn't the same as backup power. Check that a product says plainly it has a backup socket that works in a power cut, and how much it can run.
