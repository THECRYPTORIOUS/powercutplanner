Can a power station run a gas boiler?

Updated 15 November 2026

Short answer

Usually, yes. A gas boiler needs electricity for its controls, fan and pump, typically around 100 W while it's heating. The catch is that most boilers are wired in rather than plugged in, so you need an electrician to set it up before a power cut, not during one.

Why your gas boiler stops in a power cut

Gas still reaches your home in a power cut, but a modern boiler can't burn it without electricity. The controls, ignition, fan and circulation pump all need power, so the heating and hot water stop as soon as the electricity goes.

How much power it needs

Most domestic gas boilers draw somewhere around 80 to 150 W while firing, and much less when idle. On that basis, a 1 kWh power station could keep a boiler going for about 8 hours of heating time. Your boiler's manual lists its electrical power use if you want an exact figure.

The catch: it's probably not on a plug

Most boilers are connected to a fused spur, a switched outlet wired straight into the home's circuits. You can't plug that into a power station.

A qualified electrician can fit a plug-and-socket connection so you can unplug the boiler from the mains and plug it into a power station when you need to. Ask for this to be done well before winter.

Never wire a power station into your home's circuits yourself, and never use a cable with a plug on both ends to feed power back through a wall socket. It can kill you, and it can kill engineers working on the lines outside. A whole-home connection needs a proper changeover switch fitted by an electrician.

Things to check

  • Pure sine wave output. Boiler electronics need clean power. The main brands' current models produce a pure sine wave, but check before you buy a budget unit.
  • Heat pumps are different. A heat pump draws several kilowatts, which is far beyond what a portable power station can supply for long.
  • Electric heaters drain batteries fast. A 2 kW heater would empty a 1 kWh power station in under half an hour.
